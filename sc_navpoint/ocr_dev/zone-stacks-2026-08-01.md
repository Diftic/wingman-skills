## 5. What this evidence says about the scale rule

Over the 652 container rows where ALL THREE axes decoded, the
magnitudes fall into two clumps with a real gap between them: the largest
non-body container is **1.8500 km** (`glaciemring_segment_wtn-018`,
preflight) and the smallest body-scale container is **414.0045 km**
(`pyro3`, surface-01). So on fully readable rows the gap is not 2 km to
100 km, it is **1.85 km to 414 km**, and 50 km sits inside it.

Three things complicate that, all measured:

1. **The 2 km edge is a cluster edge, not empty space.** The three ring-
   station segments observed sit at 1.4624 km (`wtn-014`, game-mid),
   1.8500 km (`wtn-018`, preflight) and, on partially readable rows,
   at least 2.1450 km (`wtn-013`, starfield). Station containers are
   pressed right up against the 2 km boundary from both sides, so the
   lower edge of the assumed empty band has no margin in it.
2. **A jump point is a body-scale container that is not a body.** In
   dark-01 `frame_0001` the stack is ship -> `?umppoint_pyro_ny?` ->
   SolarSystem -> Root, with the jump-point container's own Pos at
   597.3760 km. `overlay_fields._is_body_scale` returns True for that row
   (largest component 590.6951km against a 50 km threshold), so the
   shipped gate calls a ship sitting in open space near the Pyro-Nyx jump
   point a body surface and refuses the capture. The player is not on a
   body and the frame is not rotating with one.
3. **The login screen produces a container-free stack at metre scale.**
   login and login-black decode 5/5 frames as `CamDir` ->
   `Zone: SolarSystem_200000000001 Pos: -0.00m 0.04m 1.74m` -> `Zone: Root`
   with the same values, every row trusted, nothing above SolarSystem.
   That is a synthetic system id and a 1.74 m position that passes the
   space branch cleanly, since there is genuinely no OOC line to miss.

## 6. Where this evidence is thin or ambiguous

- **Container names are frequently unreadable, coordinates usually are
  not.** `?ransit?arria?e_ele?_util_...`, `?an?ar_SmallFront_...` and
  `????_?lipper_...` never decode their leading capitals, because `C`,
  `H`, `T` and the ship-prefix letters are missing from the glyph bank at
  one or both phases. Any rule keyed on the container NAME is therefore
  building on the least readable part of the row. Rules keyed on the
  container's Pos are building on the most readable part.
- **The ship in bright-01/02, camdir-01, dark-01, flight-01, fov-01,
  starfield and terrain-01 is one ship**, entity id `742164815970`, and
  the rest-stop is one rest stop. The corpus samples few distinct places,
  so the container table is broad in frames and narrow in locations.
- **`P5_?2` at ~28,000 km is a single location** (the Pyro V rest stop's
  parent container), seen in red-01 and flight-01 only, and its name never
  decodes fully. It is the only observed container between 1,000 km and
  100,000 km.
- **Row classification here is wildcard-aware and that materially changed
  the answer.** A literal `Zone:` match reported starfield and vote-fast
  as having NO container above SolarSystem, because their label decoded
  as `Zone?` and `Z?ne:`. Reading those rows is what caught it. A label
  window must still match at least 3 characters literally, so a fully
  refused row cannot invent a container.
- **Rows whose `Zone:` label is destroyed are counted as no container at
  all**, which biases the shapes toward being too SHALLOW on damaged
  frames, never too deep. preflight `frame_0002` is an instance: the
  SolarSystem row reads `?larSy?????????92724416` with no surviving label,
  so that frame contributes no stack.
- Two cosmetic decode artifacts appear in the tables and are not real
  containers: names like `pyro3?Pos:` and `????_?l?pper_742164??5970???s:`
  occur when the space before `Pos:` refuses and the token runs on.
