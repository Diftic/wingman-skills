**What they established, in order of how much they change:**

1. **The local candidate CHANGES IDENTITY MID-FLIGHT, in ordinary manoeuvres,
   measured three times.** Leaving the jump point, the jump-point row drops out
   and the SHIP becomes the candidate. Entering a body's zone, the body becomes

   out and the candidate does not change. **One ship, one system.**
3. **A second body, with its rotation measured.** Vuur at **146.472 m/s**: a
   player standing perfectly still for 66.6 s moved **0.000 m** in the body
   frame and **9,752 m** in Root. That is why a surface waypoint cannot live in
   Root alone: roughly 10 km per minute.
4. **The first jump-point stack ever parsed**, and the first jump-point drift
   pair (0 m, stationary while refuelling).
5. **Brightness decides availability**, and the player can control it: turning
   toward dark sky took the Vuur standing pose from 1 frame in 6 to 14 of 14.

**Method rule learned the hard way, now in the ledger: a pose that is not

## 5. GATE ONE: Wave 3 shutdown semantics. NEEDS LARS

`Supercalifragilistic.md` §18 Wave 3 asks the capture provider to be stopped
**and joined**. `main._stop_nav_polling` deliberately does **not** join, with a
comment saying a capture in flight can take seconds and the thread is a daemon.

**Both cannot be honoured.**

- **Join**: shutdown is deterministic and nothing is left in flight, but Wingman
  can hang on exit for the length of a capture. Measured up to **1.9 s** on
  stored frames; live, with a grab and a self-heal, likely worse.
- **Do not join**: exit is instant, but shutdown can leave work running, which
  is the thing Wave 3 exists to prevent.

**`unload` has ZERO tests today** (`grep -rn "unload" tests/` returns nothing),
so there is no established behaviour to defer to. This is a product decision
about whether the app may pause on exit. Wave 3 cannot start until it is made.

## 7. Findings that change what to build next

**The alignment fit is the cost, and the parser is free.** Measured over 1028
frames: fit **384.4 ms** (42.7% of a cold read), block decode **546.5 ms**,
semantic parse **0.1 ms**. The ledger's old "about 300 ms of a 870 ms read" was
a source-comment hypothesis and measured low.

**The self-heal is a compounding loss.** A warm read that decodes nothing
trusted refits and decodes a SECOND time: **1266 ms** against a warm **468 ms**,
firing on **196 of 958** decoded frames. It fires on the scenes that already
refuse: 80% on `starfield`/`vote-fast`, 0% on `surface-01`. The frames least
likely to give a position are the most expensive to fail at.

**The plan's 5 Hz cadence gate is out of reach with this reader.** The warm lane
is 2.3x over the 200 ms budget before any grab, 6.3x on a self-healing frame,
and it is CPU-bound on one core. §18 Wave 5 accepts an evidence-backed
bottleneck report in place of the target; this is it.

**Brightness is NOT monotonic**, which corrects a claim made mid-session.
`red-01` at background 52.0 accepts 74% while `starfield` at 53.0 accepts 0%,
because `starfield` is STRUCTURED rather than bright. **Any preprocessing aimed
only at bright backgrounds should be expected to leave structured cases
unfixed.**

**An off-the-shelf OCR engine has NEVER been evaluated.** There is no mention of
Tesseract, EasyOCR, PaddleOCR or any established engine anywhere in this skill:
not in the plans, not in the Devlog, not in `REFUTED.md`. Lars raised this
himself. The bespoke matcher costs 1.7 to 4.5 s per read, slower than the Vision
call it replaced, so only the per-character refusal safety argument remains. The
test set for a bake-off now exists: today's four flights, across bright and dark,
with the current reader's per-frame output as the baseline to beat.
